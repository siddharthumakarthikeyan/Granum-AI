Granum documentation

Import data, inspect samples, improve labels, train models and compare results. Find guides, Python references and feature availability in one place.

Granum connects images, annotations, human review and model measurements. The documentation starts with a real small dataset and explains not only what to click, but why, what to look for, what the result means and when not to trust a conclusion.

How this documentation is organised

Guided aerial-data course is the beginner path. It introduces the concepts, follows exact actions and gives checkpoints. It records two genuinely weak GPU runs and approves only two inspected images; it does not pretend that a successful workflow creates a production model or a fully reviewed sample.

Start with Granum is the short installation and orientation reference. The complete hands-on lessons live in the course so there is one coherent first-use path rather than competing quick starts.

Concepts explains the model behind the product — what a set is, what a version is, what makes a dataset version different from the images you are looking at, and how Granum keeps a metric attached to the image it was measured on. These pages are short and worth reading once; they save you from guessing later.

Bring data in, Explore and diagnose, Review and improve, and Train and measure follow the working loop. Each guide explains its inputs, actions, saved result and current limits.

Connect your training code covers framework adapters. Build with Python contains table, schema, transform, run and collector guides. Run your workspace covers configuration, sharing and operations.

Not yet included reserves a visible place for planned capabilities. The feature index also marks partial support so that a working component is not confused with an entire completed workflow.

Reference is for looking things up: every screen, every keyboard shortcut, every import check, the models you can train, where files live on disk, what scales and what does not.

Help covers what to do when something is wrong, the questions people ask most, and exactly what leaves your machine.

Read, watch, then check

The course assumes no previous Granum experience. It explains boxes, splits and common metrics before using them. Videos are silent, captioned and accompanied by transcripts; screenshots open at full size. Every lesson separates an observed result from what it does not prove.

Guided aerial-data course

Start with a real sample. Every lesson explains the action, its evidence and its limits, with actual screenshots and captioned recordings.

  • Start the guided courseLearn Granum on a real aerial dataset: import, inspect, review, train, compare, approve, export and restore, with recorded examples.
  • 1. Prepare the workspaceCheck the installed build, download and verify the real sample, and separate source data, project records and backups.
  • 2. Understand the sampleLearn images, boxes, classes and splits, then examine the real taxonomy and duplicated-holdout problems in the supplied export.
  • 3. Import with preflightImport both splits, read the real findings, choose duplicate-class and ignore-region handling, and verify the written result.
  • 4. Explore before changing labelsUse the gallery, statistics, object patches, fields and Health to form testable questions rather than automatic cleanup rules.
  • 5. Freeze an exploratory baselinePreserve the full 120-image input before editing, and see why an immutable version is not the same thing as an approved release.
  • 6. Review, edit and recoverInspect two real images, practise recovery without leaving a false label, and separate annotation saves from human review decisions.
  • 7. Train the first modelTrain a deliberately small YOLO baseline on the frozen 96/24 input, understand the recipe, and check that the run really finished.
  • 8. Read the model evidenceInterpret runs, sample dynamics, evaluation objects and findings using the actual weak baseline, including its zero operating-point scores.
  • 9. Make a controlled comparisonChange the training schedule from three to twelve epochs on the same frozen data, then inspect compatibility, actual deltas and uncertainty.
  • 10. Approve and export an excerptApprove exactly the two inspected images and export a portable COCO copy, without calling the remaining sample reviewed or the model ready.
  • 11. Back up, restore and share safelyStop writers, verify media, create a portable project snapshot and inspect a separate restored workspace before trusting the backup.
  • 12. Move to your own projectTurn the course into a repeatable team practice with a labelling policy, independent evaluation data, review criteria and rollout evidence.

Explore and diagnose

Move from a distribution or model error to the individual samples behind it.

  • Browse imagesFilters, ordering, annotations, and the full-screen viewer with per-class isolation.
  • Inspect tables and runsUse linked rows, filters and charts to investigate the samples behind your data and metrics.
  • Build linked chartsPlot metrics, inspect images and select observations in two or three dimensions.
  • Calculate derived columnsCreate numeric, grouped and ordered measurements without changing the source table.
  • Save an analysis workflowReuse calculations, charts, filters and layout settings across compatible tables and runs.
  • Explore embedding spaceCollect feature vectors and project them into a shared two- or three-dimensional coordinate system.
  • Follow samples across epochsConnect repeated observations and measure changes without losing sample identity.
  • Find duplicates leakage and outliersReview near-identical pictures, split leakage and unusual samples before trusting an evaluation.
  • Check dataset healthCombine import, duplication, annotation and review evidence before creating a training version.
  • Inspect model errorsRead confusion, per-class results, operating thresholds and the individual objects behind each metric.

  • Edit annotationsDraw, move, relabel and delete boxes, masks and keypoints — and what each save writes.
  • Bounding boxes and rotated boxesImport, edit and measure ordinary detection boxes and oriented object annotations.
  • Instance and semantic masksImport mask labels, correct pixels and choose an export that preserves the information you need.
  • Keypoints and posePreserve landmark order, visibility and identity through import, editing, augmentation and evaluation.
  • Draft labels with a modelGenerate annotation suggestions, preserve their provenance and review them before approving a dataset.
  • Propose masks with SAMGuide segmentation with points or an object box, then inspect and apply the proposed mask.
  • Control sample weightsExclude or emphasise training samples while preserving the underlying dataset and evaluation rules.
  • Isolate, remove and restoreSet images aside, take them out of a set, and put them back.
  • Create a dataset versionFreeze what you agreed on into the named dataset that training uses.
  • AugmentationGenerate augmented copies into a dataset version, and see exactly what the model will see.
  • Export a dataset versionChoose a target annotation format and media strategy, then verify the exported data.

  • Read a runScores per round, the framework-neutral final score, and where each number comes from.
  • Per-image learningWhich images the model learned early, late, unstably or never — and what to do with each group.
  • Work the Findings queueA ranked list of labels worth checking, the evidence for each, and a decision you record.
  • Compare two runsImproved, regressed, unchanged and unmatched images — and what the comparison is worth.

Connect your training code

Use Granum with your model framework and keep predictions linked to the correct source samples.

  • Connect a PyTorch loopUse table views, weighted samplers and explicit metric passes with an existing PyTorch model.
  • Connect YOLO trainingCollect predictions and per-sample metrics from Ultralytics detection, mask, pose and rotated-box runs.
  • Connect Hugging FacePreserve sample identity through Datasets and collect Trainer predictions or hidden-state embeddings.
  • Connect LightningRecord validation and test outputs from Lightning while preserving source sample identities.
  • Connect Detectron2Register Granum tables and collect detection, instance-mask or keypoint predictions with a Detectron2 evaluator.
  • Connect SuperGradientsCollect indexed evaluation results and canonical YOLO-NAS detection or pose predictions.
